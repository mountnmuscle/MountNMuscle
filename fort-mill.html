What does the Town of Fort Mill pick up at the curb, and what will it not take?

Town of Fort Mill sanitation service includes household garbage weekly in a 95-gallon roll cart, recycling every two weeks, weekly yard debris and leaf collection, and curbside collection of bulk household items such as furniture and appliances once per week. For a couch or a dresser that you can get to the curb yourself, that weekly bulk pickup is genuinely the cheapest option and we will tell you so.

The exclusions are where people get stuck. The town does not collect hazardous materials, construction debris or electronics as part of standard service. That covers a lot of what actually accumulates in a fast-growing town: demo debris from a bathroom or flooring job, old tube TVs and monitors, paint, chemicals, and anything a contractor generated rather than a household. York County runs a Household Hazardous Material Collection and Shredding event on the first Saturday of March and October, 8am to noon, which helps for paint and chemicals but only twice a year.

York County collection and recycling centers are for York County residents dropping off household waste, and county ordinance bars private garbage collectors from using them — non-household waste has to go to a licensed landfill. That is a real constraint on how a hauler operates here, and it is built into our trailer pricing: your load goes to a licensed disposal site, not a county convenience center.

One more Fort Mill-specific wrinkle: plenty of homes with a Fort Mill mailing address sit outside the town limits in unincorporated York County. If that is you, town curbside bulk pickup does not apply at all, and your options are a private hauler or hauling it yourself.

  • Not collected by the town: hazardous materials
  • Not collected by the town: construction and demolition debris
  • Not collected by the town: electronics
  • Outside town limits: no town curbside service, town or county rules do not cover you

How much does TV mounting cost in Fort Mill?

TV mounting in Fort Mill is $99 for TVs up to 60 inches, $149 for 61 to 84 inches, $239 for 85 to 95 inches, and from $349 for 96 inches and up, which is a two-person minimum job. Each additional TV on the same visit gets 25% off mount labor, which is how most relocation households do it — get the living room, primary bedroom and bonus room done in one trip instead of three.

Add-ons are priced separately and are never discounted: stick-on cord concealment $39, in-wall cord concealment from $89, flat mount $40, full-motion mount $65. Prices assume you already have your own bracket unless you buy a mount from us. The $29 Zone 2 trip fee applies.

Fort Mill throws two specific problems at TV installs. The newer inventory in Massey, Waterside and Brayden often has a two-story great room with a stone or brick fireplace and the outlet nowhere near where the TV goes — that is the over-fireplace rate plus a cord concealment decision. The apartments and townhomes around Kingsley and Baxter Village have shared walls and lease language about wall damage, which is exactly where in-wall concealment or a stick-on channel matters more than the mount itself.
