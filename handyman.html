How much does a handyman cost in Charlotte, NC?

Handyman and assembly work is billed at $70 per hour with a two-hour minimum, so $140 is the smallest ticket. After the first two hours the clock runs in 30-minute increments, so a two-and-a-half hour visit is $175 and a three-hour visit is $210. If you would rather not watch a clock, ask for a flat per-task quote instead and we will price the job as a whole.

The two-hour minimum stays $140 on weekends. It does not climb on a Saturday or Sunday. Small handyman and assembly work is deliberately the job we want to fill a weekend with, so the weekend price is the same as the weekday price.

A few jobs genuinely need two people, and those run $135 per hour with a two-hour minimum, $270. The weekend two-person minimum is three hours, which is $405, so if a job can be done solo it is almost always cheaper to keep it solo. Typical two-person work is a wall bed anchored into framing, a heavy armoire that has to be carried upstairs before it is built, or a large playset going together outdoors.

If a job needs a trailer to carry material or haul the packaging out, a utility trailer is +$35 per hour and the 16ft enclosed trailer is +$60 per hour.

How do I make the two-hour minimum worth it?

You are paying for two hours ($140) whether you use them or not, so the single most useful thing you can do is batch. One dresser takes about 45 minutes. You have already bought the other 75 minutes, so fill them. Walk the house before you call and write down every small thing you have been ignoring, then send that list when you book.

Group by room and by tool. Everything that needs a stud finder and a drill on a wall goes in one block: TV, mirrors, art, shelves, curtain rods, baby gates. Everything that needs a screwdriver and an allen key goes in another: assembly, cabinet pulls, door hardware, hinge adjustment. Everything that needs caulk and a putty knife goes in a third. Sorting a list this way is worth real time, because the tools stay out and the ladder stays where it is.

Put the list in priority order and say so. If the visit runs long we do the top of your list first and stop where the time runs out, rather than finishing the least important thing and leaving the one that mattered. If we finish everything early, we still bill the two-hour minimum, so it is better to have one extra item on the list than one too few.

A realistic sense of what fits in two hours: roughly one large flat-pack build, or three to five small mounting tasks, or a mixed list of six to eight quick items. A wardrobe with mirrored sliding doors, a bunk bed, or a treadmill will generally consume the whole two hours on its own.

  • Batch by tool, not by room order: all drilling, then all assembly, then all caulk and patch
  • Send the full list when you book so the right tools and ladder come on the truck
  • Rank the list, top item first, so the time goes where it matters
  • Combine handyman time with flat-rate work on the same visit: TV mounting from $99 up to 60 inches, junk removal from $115 for a single item

What do I need to have on hand before you arrive?

Have the actual item in the house, unopened is fine, and have it in the room where it is going to live. Time spent carrying boxes up a flight of stairs is billed at the same $70 per hour as the assembly itself, and stairs above the first flight add $25 per flight. If the box is still in the garage and the bed goes in an upstairs bedroom, that is time on your ticket.

For a fixture swap, the fixture itself has to be there. We do not shop for parts as part of the labor rate. Buy the ceiling fan, the faucet, the toilet seat, the door lockset, the cabinet pulls. Count the pulls twice, because a kitchen with 34 doors and drawers is where people come up three short and the job stops.

Mounting hardware is the other common holdup. Most furniture comes with what it needs, but wall work often does not. Floating shelves, heavy mirrors, TV mounts and garage racks frequently ship without anchors rated for the wall you actually have. Tell us what the wall is when you book: drywall over wood studs, drywall over metal studs, plaster, brick or block. That changes the anchors and it changes how long the job takes.

Last, clear the work area and pen the dog. Move the nightstands away from the wall, take the art down off the wall we are opening up, and clear the floor space for a build. A room that is ready is often 20 to 30 minutes of billable time you keep.

  • The item, the fixture and the parts, in the room they are going in
  • All hardware counted, including extra cabinet pulls or knobs
  • Wall type known and mentioned when booking: drywall, metal stud, plaster, brick or block
  • Anchors rated for that wall if the product did not include them
  • Work area cleared, floor space open, pets secured

What handyman work needs a licensed trade instead?

There is a short list we will not touch, and it is better to know it before you book than after. New electrical circuits, panel and breaker work, gas lines, plumbing inside the wall, roofing, structural framing and HVAC all need a licensed trade in North Carolina. If your job is on that list, we will tell you on the phone and you should call an electrical or plumbing contractor directly.

The line on electrical is simpler than people expect. Swapping a fixture, a ceiling fan or a switch cover on wiring that already exists is normal handyman work. Running new wire, adding a circuit, moving or adding a receptacle, changing a breaker or touching the panel is licensed electrical contracting in North Carolina and should be done by a licensed electrical contractor who pulls the permit.

Plumbing works the same way. Replacing a faucet, a supply line, a toilet seat, a shower head or a garbage disposal on existing connections is a swap. Opening a wall, altering drain or vent lines, moving a water heater, soldering supply pipe or anything that requires a permit is licensed plumbing work.

On general contracting, North Carolina requires a general contractor license for undertakings of $40,000 or more. Ordinary handyman and assembly work sits far below that, which is why this kind of work is legal for a non-licensed operator to perform. We do not hold a general contractor, electrical or plumbing license and we do not represent ourselves as holding one. We stay inside what handyman work actually is.

If you are not sure which side of the line your job falls on, describe it on the phone. It costs nothing to ask, and it saves a trip fee and a wasted afternoon for both of us.

  • Out of scope: new circuits, panel or breaker work
  • Out of scope: gas lines of any kind
  • Out of scope: plumbing behind the wall, drain and vent alterations, water heater relocation
  • Out of scope: roofing, structural work and HVAC
  • In scope: like-for-like fixture swaps on existing wiring and existing plumbing connections
