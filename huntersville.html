How much does junk removal cost in Huntersville, NC?

A single item is $95. Volume loads are priced by the trailer: a 5x8 utility trailer runs $145 quarter full, $210 half, $285 packed; a 6x10 utility runs $170 quarter, $230 half, $335 packed. The 16ft enclosed trailer is priced in eighths at $150, $235, $315, $395, $475, $560 and $700 for a full load. The Huntersville $49 trip fee is added on top.

Most Huntersville calls land in the middle of that range rather than at either end. A bonus room cleared of a sectional, a treadmill and a decade of boxes is usually a half to packed 6x10. A full basement or a three-car garage that has quietly become storage is enclosed-trailer territory. Surcharges are flat and stated up front: mattress or box spring +$25 each, refrigerant appliance +$40, tire +$8, and paint or e-waste billed at actual disposal cost.

Where can Huntersville residents legally dump bulky items themselves?

Two routes. Huntersville residents request bulk item and brush pile pickup through Town Public Works at 704-875-7007, and a disposal fee applies to that service. For self-haul, the county's North Mecklenburg Disposal and Recycling Center sits right in town at 12300 Statesville Road, open Monday through Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4 p.m.

Curbside service is run by Waste Connections, and it is deliberately narrow. Carts go out by 7 a.m. with three feet between them and four feet of clearance from mailboxes and parked cars. Yard waste is capped at five bags of leaves a week, rising to ten bags from November 1 through January 15, with a 75-pound limit per bag. Construction debris, dirt and sod are not collected at all, which is exactly the material a bathroom demo or a deck teardown produces.

A few rules catch people out. North Carolina has banned televisions and computer equipment from landfills since July 1, 2011, so a dead 65-inch plasma cannot simply go in a dumpster. Refrigerators, freezers and window units need their refrigerant handled before disposal, which is why that +$40 line exists. And since early 2025 the North Mecklenburg center has kept business and non-residential loads and dump trailers out on Saturdays, so contractor-style loads have to move Monday through Friday. That restriction shapes our Saturday scheduling in Huntersville more than anything else does.

Do you charge a trip fee to come to Huntersville?

Yes. Huntersville is Zone 3, which carries a flat $49 trip fee per visit. It is charged once regardless of whether you booked junk removal, a TV mount or an hourly crew, and it is quoted in writing before we roll.

The reason is honest mileage. Our base is Steele Creek, in the far southwest corner of Charlotte, and Huntersville is roughly 30 miles north via I-485 and I-77 to Exit 23 at Gilead Road or Exit 25 at NC 73 / Sam Furr Road. On a good morning that is 40 minutes. On a weekday afternoon with I-77 backed up toward the Lake Norman bridges, it can be well over an hour each way, and a loaded trailer does not weave.

Practically, that means we batch north-county work. Booking a day or two ahead gets you a real arrival window; same-day is often available but genuinely harder up here than it is inside I-485, and same-day service carries a +$40 add-on. If you are flexible on the day, say so when you call. Landing your job next to another Huntersville stop is the single best way to get an early slot.

What kind of jobs do Huntersville homes actually generate?

Most of the volume comes from 1990s through 2010s subdivisions now turning over their first or second owners. Cedarfield, NorthStone, Wynfield Forest and The Hamptons are full of houses where the original family raised kids, floored the attic, filled the bonus room over the garage and is now downsizing or selling. That is the classic packed-trailer call: bedroom sets nobody wants, a treadmill, an old console TV, and thirty banker boxes.

Newer pockets behave differently. Monteith Park, Vermillion and the townhome blocks near Birkdale Village and Villages at Rosedale mean rear-loaded garages, shared alleys and tight turning radiuses. We size the trailer to the alley there, not to the load, and sometimes make two runs rather than block a neighbor in. Lake-adjacent homes off NC 73 add their own category: waterlogged dock furniture, jet ski trailers, boat covers, cracked kayaks and patio sets that have taken ten summers of sun.

HOA rules matter here more than in most of Mecklenburg County. Several Huntersville communities restrict how long anything can sit at the curb and where a trailer can park. Load-and-go on the same visit avoids the problem entirely, which is how we prefer to work anyway. If your HOA requires notice or a gate code, tell us at booking rather than at the curb.

How much is TV mounting in Huntersville?

TV mounting is flat rate: $99 for sets up to 60 inches, $149 for 61 to 75 inches, $199 for 76 to 85 inches, and from $299 for 86 inches and up, which is a two-person minimum. Mounting over a fireplace or on stone or brick starts at $199. Each additional TV on the same visit takes 25% off the mount labor.

That fireplace line comes up constantly in Huntersville. The great-room-with-stacked-stone-chimney layout is standard in Skybrook, Gilead Ridge and the Birkdale-area builds, and stone or brick means masonry anchors, a longer job and a different price than drywall-and-stud. Two-story great rooms also push the mount higher than most people expect, so a full-motion arm is usually worth it there.

Add-ons are never discounted: stick-on cord concealment $39, in-wall concealment from $89, flat mount $40, full-motion mount $65. Prices assume you already have a bracket unless you buy a mount from us. The $49 Zone 3 trip fee applies to mounting visits the same as everything else, so pairing a mount with a junk load or an hour of handyman work on one visit is the efficient play.

What does hourly labor, moving help and flooring cost here?

Solo handyman labor is $70/hr with a 2-hour minimum. A two-person crew is $135/hr, also a 2-hour minimum. Add a utility trailer for +$25/hr or the 16ft enclosed for +$60/hr, so a two-person crew with the enclosed trailer is $195/hr. After the minimum, time is billed in 30-minute increments.

Weekends work a little differently. On Saturday and Sunday the two-person hourly crew carries a 3-hour minimum, while solo handyman work stays at 2 hours. Flat-rate services like TV mounting and junk removal have no minimum at all. Sunday adds 15% to flat-rate work only, never to hourly crew work. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, which shows up in Huntersville's three-story townhomes and in walkout-basement homes on sloped lake-side lots. Heavy or awkward items start at +$40.

Flooring is priced by the square foot: LVP or laminate at $2.50/sq ft, herringbone or premium patterns at $3.50/sq ft, tear-out and haul-away at $0.50/sq ft, and subfloor prep at $70/hr. Minimum job is 200 sq ft or $500. The most common Huntersville flooring call is pulling twenty-year-old carpet out of a bonus room or a whole main level before a listing goes live, and since the old carpet and pad cannot go in a curbside cart, the tear-out and the haul leave in the same trailer.
