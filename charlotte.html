How much does junk removal cost in Charlotte?

A single item picked up anywhere in Charlotte is $95. Beyond that you pay for the space you fill: a 5x8 utility trailer is $145 quarter, $210 half, $285 packed, a 6x10 utility trailer is $170 quarter, $230 half, $335 packed, and the 16ft enclosed trailer is priced in eighths at $150 for 1/8, $235 for 1/4, $315 for 3/8, $395 for 1/2, $475 for 5/8, $560 for 3/4 and $700 full.

Certain items cost the landfill or the recycling center extra, so they carry a surcharge: mattress or box spring +$25 each, refrigerant appliance +$40 (refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, dehumidifiers), tires +$8 each, and paint or electronics billed at disposal cost because those have to be routed to a specific county facility instead of a landfill. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, which comes up constantly in the three-story townhomes around Ayrsley and South End. A heavy or awkward item such as a treadmill, a large appliance or an oversized sectional starts at +$40. Pianos, gun safes, hot tubs and slate pool tables are quoted separately and never go at the single-item rate, and same-day service is +$40.

The volume of work in Charlotte follows the housing. Apartment turnover along the South Boulevard corridor and the Blue Line stations at Arrowood, Tyvola, Woodlawn and Scaleybark generates constant end-of-lease furniture. Garage and attic cleanouts run heavy in the newer Steele Creek subdivisions off Shopton Road and South Tryon where two-car garages fill up fast. Downsizing in the 1960s and 70s ranch neighborhoods like Madison Park, Montford and Yorkmount produces the big, dense loads: console furniture, sleeper sofas, box TVs and decades of garage shelving.

Where can I legally get rid of bulky junk in Charlotte?

Charlotte residents have two legal routes: city bulky item pickup, which is appointment-only, and Mecklenburg County full-service recycling and disposal centers, open Monday through Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. The city route requires you to schedule ahead through 311, the CLT+ app or the online service request portal, then set items at the curb by 6 a.m. on the collection day with about three feet between pieces and clear of carts, mailboxes and storm drains. Setting bulky items out without an appointment gets them left behind and can draw a code complaint.

The bigger catch is what the city will not take. Construction and demolition debris, electronics, paint, batteries, tires, yard waste and household hazardous waste are all excluded from bulky pickup. On top of that, multi-family properties are on private hauler contracts, so if you live in an apartment or a managed condo building anywhere from South End to Steele Creek, you get no city bulky pickup at all and your dumpster corral is not a legal place to leave a sofa.

The county centers cover the gap. The four full-service sites are Compost Central at 140 Valleydale Road off Wilkinson Boulevard, which is the closest one to Steele Creek and the airport side, Foxhole at 17131 Lancaster Highway in south Charlotte, Hickory Grove at 8007 Pence Road on the east side, and North Mecklenburg at 12300 N. Statesville Road in Huntersville. They take bulky waste, scrap metal, yard waste, electronics and household hazardous waste, and paint and HHW are free to county residents who can show proof of residency. Bulky loads and construction debris carry per-load fees, and there are caps on free tires and on TVs per visit, so call ahead if you are hauling a lot of either.

North Carolina law also bans televisions, computers, monitors, printers and most peripherals from landfills statewide, which is why a TV is never a curbside item here. When we haul, that separation is already handled: e-waste goes to the county e-waste stream, refrigerant appliances go where the refrigerant can be recovered, and paint goes to household hazardous waste.

Do you charge a trip fee to my part of Charlotte?

South and southwest Charlotte are Zone 1 with no trip fee. Central Charlotte is Zone 2, which adds $29. That is the whole fee structure inside the city limits.

Zone 1 covers Steele Creek and everything the shop can reach without crossing the middle of the city: Berewick, the Palisades, Ayrsley, Whitehall, Yorkmount, Arrowood, Rivergate, the South Tryon and Steele Creek Road corridors, plus Ballantyne, SouthPark, Madison Park, Montford and the neighborhoods along Park Road and Providence. Zone 2 is the center of the city and the near-in historic side, including Uptown and Fourth Ward, South End, Dilworth, Wilmore, Elizabeth, Plaza Midwood, NoDa and Wesley Heights.

The $29 is about traffic and access, not distance. Uptown and South End work means loading zones, permit-parked streets, service entrances and freight elevators rather than pulling a trailer into a driveway, and a job that takes twenty minutes of staging in Berewick can take an hour of building logistics on Tryon Street. If your building requires a reserved freight elevator window or advance notice to management, book that first and tell us the window when you call, because arriving without it usually means the crew cannot get past the lobby.

How much does TV mounting cost in Charlotte?

TV mounting is flat rate: up to 60 inches is $99, 61 to 75 inches is $149, 76 to 85 inches is $199, and 86 inches and up is custom quoted with a two-person minimum. Mounting over a fireplace or onto stone or brick starts at $199. A second or third TV on the same visit gets 25 percent off the mount labor.

Add-ons are never discounted and are priced separately: stick-on cord concealment $39, in-wall cord concealment from $89, flat mount $40, full-motion mount $65. Quoted prices assume you already have your own bracket unless you buy a mount from us.

Charlotte throws three recurring wall problems at this job. The newer Steele Creek, Berewick and Ballantyne builds love a stacked stone or brick fireplace surround in a two-story great room, which means masonry anchors, a taller working height and the fireplace rate. Uptown and South End towers put you on concrete, block or steel studs where wood-stud lag bolts are useless and in-wall cord runs may be blocked by fire-rated assemblies. The older bungalows and 1920s to 1940s houses in Dilworth, Wilmore, Elizabeth and Plaza Midwood often still have plaster over wood lath, which crumbles at the edges of a bad hole and hides studs at irregular spacing, so we locate and pilot carefully rather than trusting a stud finder reading.

What does moving help or hourly labor cost in Charlotte?

A solo handyman is $70 per hour with a 2-hour minimum and a two-person crew is $135 per hour with a 2-hour minimum. Add a trailer to hourly work at +$35 per hour for a utility trailer or +$60 per hour for the 16ft enclosed, so a two-person crew with the enclosed trailer is $195 per hour. After the minimum, time is billed in 30-minute increments.

Weekends work slightly differently. On Saturday and Sunday the two-person hourly crew carries a 3-hour minimum, while the solo handyman stays at the 2-hour minimum. Flat-rate services like TV mounting and junk removal have no minimum at all on weekends. Sunday adds 15 percent to flat-rate work only and never to hourly crew work.

Typical Charlotte labor calls: loading or unloading a rental truck at an apartment on the South Boulevard corridor, moving heavy pieces between floors in a three-story townhome, clearing a garage before a listing photo shoot, shuffling furniture for flooring or paint, and getting a sectional or armoire up a narrow bungalow staircase. Stairs above the first flight are +$25 per flight, and single heavy or awkward items start at +$40. For uptown high-rises, confirm your building's move-in window, freight elevator reservation and any paperwork management requires before you book the crew, since that is the single most common reason a downtown job stalls.

Can you install LVP or laminate flooring in a Charlotte home?

Flooring installation is $2.50 per square foot for LVP and laminate and $3.50 per square foot for herringbone and premium patterns. Tear-out and haul-away of the old floor is $1.00 per square foot, subfloor prep is $70 per hour, and there is a minimum of 200 square feet or $500, whichever is greater.

Two Charlotte housing patterns drive most of this work. The 1990s and 2000s builds across Steele Creek, Whitehall, Yorkmount and Highland Creek went down as carpet over plywood in living areas and vinyl in wet rooms, and those are straightforward pull-and-replace jobs where the tear-out line item does most of the heavy lifting. Slab-on-grade ranches and townhomes are a different animal: slabs move and dish over time, so a floor that telegraphs every low spot usually needs leveling compound billed at the subfloor prep rate rather than more underlayment.

In the older stock closer to center city, expect surprises under the carpet. Original hardwood worth saving, multiple layers of previous flooring, and transitions that were never square are all common in pre-war houses. If your home is inside one of Charlotte's local historic districts, exterior and structural changes go through the Historic District Commission, but interior floor replacement generally does not, which is why flooring is one of the easier upgrades in those neighborhoods.
