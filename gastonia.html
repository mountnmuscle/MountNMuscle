How much does junk removal cost in Gastonia?

A single item pickup in Gastonia is $115. Trailer loads run $145 quarter, $210 half and $285 packed on the 5x8 utility trailer, and $170 quarter, $230 half and $335 packed on the 6x10 utility. Gastonia is Zone 3, so add the $49 trip fee to any of those numbers.

For whole-house and estate volume we bring the 16ft enclosed trailer, priced by how much of the box you fill: 1/8 $150, 1/4 $235, 3/8 $315, 1/2 $395, 5/8 $475, 3/4 $560, full $700. Most single-room cleanouts in a York-Chester bungalow or a Gardner Park ranch land somewhere between a quarter and a half of the enclosed trailer.

Item surcharges are added on top: mattress or box spring +$25 each, refrigerant appliance +$40, tire +$8, and paint or e-waste at whatever the disposal site charges us. Carry-outs from above the first floor add $25 per flight, a genuinely heavy or awkward piece starts at +$40, and same-day service is +$40 when we can fit it.

The east-side subdivisions toward Charlotte produce garage cleanouts, playsets, treadmills and post-renovation debris.

What will the City of Gastonia pick up at the curb, and what won't it take?

The City of Gastonia collects appliances and electronics at the curb for $25 per item — washers, dryers, refrigerators, water heaters, stoves, dishwashers, microwaves, HVAC units, TVs, computers and monitors. Set the item at the curb before your regular collection day, then call the Customer Care Center at 704-866-6714 to report it; the city's stated window is pickup within 72 hours.

Ordinary bulky household trash — furniture, mattresses, a lawn mower — is charged by weight, commonly $15 for items under 100 pounds and $25 for heavier ones. Tires are $5 off the rim and $8 on the rim. Twice a year, usually one week in the spring and one in the fall, the city runs a Free Excess Trash Week that waives the bulk fee, though appliances, electronics and tires still carry their charge because state law keeps them out of the landfill. Confirm current fees and the next free week at 704-866-6714 before you plan around them.

The city will not take auto parts or batteries, dirt, rock, concrete, brick or ceramic tile, construction and demolition debris, liquids, poisons, explosives, or wet paint cans. Those belong at the Gaston County Landfill at 3155 Philadelphia Church Road in Dallas, generally open Monday through Friday and Saturday mornings. Recycling drop-off is at 3175 Duke Street and at the Gaston County Farmers Market on East Long Street, with county recycling centers on North Broad Street and Camp Street; the county recycling hotline is 704-922-7729.

Yard waste has its own rules that catch people. Limbs are accepted up to 4 inches in diameter and 5 feet in length, loose piles have to be under about 50 pounds and no more than 4 feet high, and stumps, root balls and trunks over 4 inches are refused outright. Plastic bags are not accepted. Loose piles are free up to roughly a pickup load, with a charge above 4.5 cubic yards. Floor covering has to be cut into 4-foot lengths, rolled and tied.

If you can get the item to the curb and you can wait, the city route is almost always the cheaper option and we will say so. Call us when the item cannot get to the curb on its own — a sleeper sofa on the second floor of a mill house, a water heater in a crawlspace, a shed full of scrap in a back yard with no alley access — or when the pile includes the tile, brick, concrete and renovation debris the city refuses.

Can you mount a TV on the brick and plaster walls in Gastonia's older homes?

TV mounting in Gastonia is $99 for sets up to 60 inches, $149 for 61-84 inches, $239 for 85-95 inches, and from $349 for 96 inches and up, which is a two-person minimum. Mounting over a fireplace or into stone or brick starts at $199. The $49 trip fee applies.

Wall construction varies more here than in a subdivision-only town, and it changes the job. York-Chester and Brookwood houses are frequently plaster over wood lath with true dimensional studs behind it, which is solid to anchor into but unforgiving if you guess at stud location. Mill village houses in the Loray district — the blocks around Firestone Street and Vance Street — are early-1900s frame construction with irregular stud spacing and shallow wall cavities that often rule out an in-wall power kit. The Loray Mill Lofts and the converted upper floors along Main Avenue are brick and heavy timber, so masonry anchors and surface cord routing are the realistic answer.

Add-ons are never discounted: stick-on cord concealment $39, in-wall cord kit from $89, flat mount $40, full-motion mount $65. A second TV on the same visit gets 25% off the mount labor. Pricing assumes you already own the bracket unless you buy a mount from us.

What does hourly moving and labor help cost in Gastonia?

A solo handyman is $70 per hour with a 2-hour minimum, and a two-person crew is $135 per hour with a 2-hour minimum. Adding the utility trailer is +$35/hr and the 16ft enclosed is +$60/hr, so a two-person crew with the enclosed trailer runs $195/hr. After the minimum we bill in 30-minute increments.

Weekends work a little differently. Saturday and Sunday two-person hourly crews carry a 3-hour minimum; the solo handyman stays at the 2-hour minimum. Flat-rate services like TV mounting and junk removal have no minimum at all, and the Sunday 15% surcharge applies only to that flat-rate work, never to hourly crew time.

Gastonia's older housing stock is what drives the labor line. Mill village stairs are narrow, steep and often turn at a cramped landing, original doorways are undersized, and a queen box spring or a long sectional frequently will not make the turn without being stood on end or taken apart. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first and a heavy or awkward piece starts at +$40. Downtown loft moves have to be scheduled around elevator and loading-dock access. We also do load-and-unload help for people moving between Gastonia and Charlotte, where you rent the truck and we supply the muscle at both ends of I-85.

Do you install flooring in Gastonia mill houses and older homes?

LVP and laminate installation is $2.50 per square foot, herringbone and premium patterns are $3.50 per square foot, tear-out and haul-away is $1.00 per square foot, and subfloor prep is $70 per hour. The minimum job is 200 square feet or $500.

In the pre-war housing around Loray, York-Chester and the older streets off West Franklin, the floor under the carpet is usually original tongue-and-groove pine over joists that have settled unevenly across a hundred-plus years of crawlspace humidity. Expect high spots at hearths and thresholds and a real subfloor prep line on the quote rather than a surprise on install day. Mid-century ranches in Gardner Park and Southwood are typically plywood or slab and go faster. Newer builds on the east side toward Charlotte are builder carpet over clean plywood, which is the most predictable of the three.

Because we haul, the tear-out leaves with us at $1.00 per square foot — carpet, pad, tack strip and staples included. That matters locally, since the city requires floor covering to be cut into 4-foot lengths, rolled and tied before it will take it at the curb, and old carpet from a full house usually exceeds the free bulk allowance anyway.

We cover all of Gastonia, and because the city is in Zone 3 a $49 trip fee is added to every job here on top of the service price. It is stated up front on the quote, not tacked on afterward.

Practically, that means the whole grid inside the city: the Loray Mill village and the FUSE District blocks around CaroMont Health Park, downtown and the Main Avenue storefronts and upper-floor units, York-Chester and Brookwood, the Franklin Boulevard corridor, the neighborhoods north of I-85, Gardner Park, Robinwood, Riverwood, Fern Forest, Hudson Woods and Southwood, the newer east-side subdivisions like Robinson Oaks, Bethesda Oaks, Cramer Woods and Eagles Walk, and the streets running southwest toward Crowders Mountain State Park.
