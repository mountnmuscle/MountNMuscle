How much does junk removal cost in Mount Holly, NC?

A single item is $95, and a loaded 5x8 utility trailer runs $145 for a quarter load, $210 for a half, and $285 packed. The 6x10 utility trailer is $170 quarter, $230 half, $335 packed. For a whole-house cleanout — the kind an old mill cottage or a long-held family home off Woodlawn Avenue generates — the 16ft enclosed trailer prices by fraction: $150 for 1/8, $235 for 1/4, $315 for 3/8, $395 for 1/2, $475 for 5/8, $560 for 3/4, and $700 for a full load.

Surcharges are added per item, not baked into a vague quote. Mattresses and box springs are +$25 each, refrigerant appliances (fridges, freezers, window units, dehumidifiers) are +$40, tires are +$8 each, and paint and e-waste are billed at whatever the disposal site charges. Stairs above the first flight add $25, a heavy or awkward item like a gun safe or an upright piano starts at +$40, and same-day service is +$40 when we can fit it. Mount Holly is Zone 2, so add the $29 trip fee. We are often available same day, but we will not promise a window we cannot hold.

What will the City of Mount Holly pick up, and what do I have to haul myself?

Mount Holly runs a paid sticker program: you go to the Municipal Complex, pay a $15 flat fee per standard bulk item (couches, chairs, desks), get an orange collection sticker, and set the item at the curb on your regular garbage day. Appliances are $25, mattresses $20, box springs $5, and tires off the rim $5 each. Payment is cash, check, or money order — no card. Televisions and computer monitors are not accepted in the bulk program at all.

That last part is where most Mount Holly calls to us start. A TV, a monitor, a pile of old electronics, or a load that is bigger than a sticker or two makes the sticker route slow and awkward. So does anything you cannot physically get to the curb — which describes a lot of second-floor furniture in the older houses near Highland Street and South Chestnut. City curbside recycling has also been suspended since July 1, 2024, so recyclables now go to the Riverbend Recycling Center at 146 Mountain Island Road, and larger volumes go to the Gaston County landfill on Philadelphia Church Road over in Dallas. Yard waste stays separate: 32-gallon cans or clear bags marked YARD WASTE, with limbs and shrub clippings stacked at the curb rather than dumped in the street.

If the sticker program covers what you have and you can get it curbside, use it — it is cheaper than us. Call us when the item is upstairs, when it is a TV or monitor, when there are ten items instead of two, or when you need it gone before the next collection day.

Can you get furniture out of an old Mount Holly mill house with narrow stairs?

Yes, and that is most of what the Riverfront and downtown blocks generate. Labor is $70/hr for a solo handyman or $135/hr for a two-person crew, both with a 2-hour minimum, billed in 30-minute increments after that. Add a utility trailer for +$25/hr or the 16ft enclosed for +$60/hr, which puts a two-person crew with the big trailer at $195/hr.

Mount Holly's housing stock around the 1875 mill and the A&E plant was built for mill families, not for modern sectionals. Doorways are narrow, stair runs are steep and often turn at a landing, hallways are tight, and a lot of these homes never had a wide entry to begin with. Craftsman bungalows from the 1920s and 1930s on Riverside Drive and the streets off Elm Avenue have the same issue. Real work in these houses means measuring the turn before lifting, taking legs and doors off, sometimes taking a door off its hinges, and knowing when a piece has to be disassembled rather than forced. Stairs above the first flight add $25 per flight, and a genuinely heavy or awkward item starts at +$40.

The rental stock in and around downtown drives a second kind of job: turnovers. Tenant left a couch, a mattress, and half a garage. That is usually a trailer-load flat rate rather than hourly.

What does TV mounting cost in Mount Holly?

TV mounting is $99 for sets up to 60 inches, $149 for 61-75 inches, $199 for 76-85 inches, and from $299 for 86 inches and up (that size is a two-person minimum). Mounting over a fireplace or into stone or brick starts at $199. A second TV on the same visit gets 25% off the mount labor.

Brick and stone come up constantly here. The older homes near Main Street and the mill village blocks were built with real masonry chimneys and brick fireplace surrounds, and drilling into brick or fieldstone is a different job than hitting a stud in drywall — different bits, different anchors, more time, and no room to be wrong about placement. Newer construction in Fites Creek Plantation, Kendrick Farm, Stonewater, and the developments strung along Mount Holly-Huntersville Road is mostly stud-and-drywall with a pre-wired outlet, which is the fast version.

Add-ons are never discounted: stick-on cord concealment is $39, in-wall cord routing starts at $89, a flat mount is $40, and a full-motion mount is $65. Prices assume you already have a bracket unless you buy a mount from us.

Do you install flooring in Mount Holly homes?

Yes. LVP and laminate are $2.50 per square foot, herringbone and premium patterns are $3.50 per square foot, tear-out and haul-away of the old floor is $0.50 per square foot, and subfloor prep is $70/hr. There is a minimum of 200 square feet or $500.

Subfloor prep matters more in Mount Holly than in a 2019 subdivision. Century-old mill houses and the mid-century homes around Catawba Heights sit on crawlspaces, have settled, and often carry two or three layers of history — original pine, then sheet vinyl, then a floating floor somebody put down in 2006. Squeaks, soft spots near old plumbing runs, and a floor that is out of level get found and priced before we start laying plank, not after. Because tear-out and haul are quoted separately, you see exactly what the demo and disposal cost versus the install.

New-build owners along the river and up toward Mountain Island Lake usually call for the opposite reason: swapping builder-grade carpet for LVP across a great room and hall, where the subfloor is fine and the job is straightforward square footage.

Do you actually cover Mount Holly, or just Charlotte?

We cover Mount Holly as a regular part of the route, not as a favor. We are based in Steele Creek in southwest Charlotte, and Mount Holly is one Catawba River crossing away — up I-485 to I-85 across the river, or straight over on NC-27 from the Charlotte side. That is why Mount Holly is Zone 2 with a flat $29 trip fee rather than something higher.

Weekend rules are worth knowing before you book. On Saturday and Sunday, the two-person hourly crew carries a 3-hour minimum; the solo handyman stays at the 2-hour minimum. Flat-rate work — TV mounting and junk removal — has no minimum at all. A Sunday adds 15% to flat-rate work only, never to hourly crew work.

Payment is cash, check, or Zelle with no fee; cards carry a 3.5% processing fee. Our Friends & Neighbors referral program gives the person who refers you a $25 credit and takes $25 off your first booking. Three booked referrals earns a $100 credit or a free hour of solo labor.
