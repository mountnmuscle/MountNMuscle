Do you do junk removal in Lake Wylie, SC?

Yes, and Lake Wylie is Zone 1 with no trip fee — a single item is $115 whether the house is on Charlotte Highway or down a long private drive off Bethel School Road. We are based in Steele Creek, on the Mecklenburg side of the Buster Boyd Bridge, so Lake Wylie is one of the shortest runs we make. Crossing SC 49 into York County costs you nothing extra.

The bigger thing to understand about Lake Wylie is that it is not a town. It is an unincorporated community in York County, so there is no city hall, no municipal public works department, and no town-run curbside bulk pickup to schedule. Your household trash comes from a private subscription hauler, and anything larger than a trash cart is your problem to solve. That single fact is why most of our Lake Wylie calls exist: there is no free truck coming for the old sectional.

We cover the whole SC 49 and SC 274 side of the lake — the older waterfront streets in River Hills, the newer build-outs at Paddlers Cove, Handsmill and Cypress Point, the smaller established pockets like Timberlake, Riverpointe and Carolina Coves, and the acreage properties out toward Crowders Creek and Clover.

Where can Lake Wylie residents legally get rid of bulky items themselves?

The closest legal drop-off is the York County Lake Wylie Collection & Recycling Center at 102 Bethel School Road in Clover. It is for York County residents dropping household quantities, not for contractor loads or remodel debris, hours are limited, Saturday is a short day and the centers are closed Sunday. Bring proof of York County residency.

For anything past a carload, the York County landfill fee schedule is what applies. County residents can dispose of up to one ton per month at no charge with proof of residency. Beyond that, household waste runs $49 per ton with a $5 minimum, construction and demolition debris $42 per ton, white goods and scrap metal $20 per ton, and car and truck tires $75 per ton with a $10 minimum. Bulky items needing special handling are charged at a higher per-ton rate, and an unsecured load in a passenger vehicle carries a $25 penalty. County fees change, so call before you load up.

Driving your junk across the bridge into Charlotte is not the shortcut it looks like on a map. Mecklenburg County's recycling and disposal centers charge out-of-county users at commercial rates unless they buy an annual out-of-county sticker, so a York County address does not get you the resident deal. Refrigerators, freezers and window units also need their refrigerant recovered by a certified technician before disposal, which is what our +$40 refrigerant surcharge covers.

If none of that sounds like how you want to spend a Saturday, that is the job. We load it, we haul it, we handle the disposal end.

What kind of junk do Lake Wylie waterfront homes actually generate?

Lake Wylie work skews heavy, awkward and outdoors. Waterfront lots on the Catawba produce a specific kind of debris: rotted dock boards and railing sections after a repair, waterlogged foam dock floats, torn boat and pontoon covers, split kayaks and paddleboards, cracked plastic slides, worn-out patio furniture, old grills, life-jacket bins, and rusted boat trailers. We haul that from the shoreline or the dock approach up to the trailer; we do not do in-water work or shoreline demolition, and anything touching the shoreline itself falls under Duke Energy lake use permitting.

The housing stock splits in a way that shows up in the work. River Hills is the oldest and largest planned waterfront community on the lake, and those homes are now decades into their second and third owners — that means dated built-ins, basement full of prior-owner leftovers, garage-full estate clearouts, and old console TVs and CRTs nobody wants. The newer subdivisions off SC 49 and SC 274, built through the population surge that took Lake Wylie from roughly 8,800 residents in 2010 to over 13,600 in 2020, generate the opposite: moving-in junk. Packing debris, builder trash, the old furniture that did not survive the downsize from a Charlotte house, and a TV that needs to go up on the great room wall this weekend.

Second homes and lake rentals add turnover work — full furniture swaps between seasons, mattress replacements in bulk, and mounting TVs in bedrooms that were never wired for it. Big lots and long driveways help us, because we can usually back a trailer close to the garage instead of shuttling loads a hundred yards.

Gated and HOA communities have their own rules. River Hills has staffed gates with barcode-only access on the West Gate, so vendors come through the front gate — tell us ahead of time and we will handle it. Many Lake Wylie HOAs also prohibit trailers or piles sitting in yards and driveways, which is the practical reason people call rather than staging junk out front for a week.
