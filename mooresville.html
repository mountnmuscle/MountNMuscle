A single item is $115, and Mooresville carries an $89 Zone 4 trip fee on top of whatever the job prices out to. Everything larger than one item is priced by how much trailer space it fills, not by guesswork at the curb.

The 5x8 utility trailer is $145 for a quarter load, $210 for a half and $285 packed. The 6x10 utility runs $170 quarter, $230 half and $335 packed. Why is there an $89 trip fee for Mooresville, and can you come the same day?

The fee is $89 because Mooresville is Zone 4, the outer edge of the service area, and the base is in Steele Creek on the far southwest corner of Charlotte. That is the width of Mecklenburg County plus the Lake Norman corridor before the truck and trailer ever reach your driveway.

Same-day is possible as a +$40 add-on, but the honest answer for Mooresville is that it rarely works out. I-77 north bunches up through the Huntersville and Cornelius stretch, and the express lanes only run as far as Exit 36, so anything past the afternoon build-up turns a 45-minute drive into something much longer. Booking a day or more ahead is how you get a real arrival window instead of a maybe.

Distance also shapes how the day is planned. A Mooresville job is usually built as the anchor of a run, not squeezed between two Charlotte stops, which is another reason a scheduled slot beats a last-minute call. If you are on the Brawley School Road peninsula, that goes double: there is effectively one road on and off the peninsula, and school release and summer lake traffic can add half an hour by themselves.

What will the Town of Mooresville haul away for free, and what won't they take?

Town of Mooresville sanitation picks up bulky waste every week on your regular trash day at no extra charge, with no appointment, which makes it the right first call for a lot of items. The town accepts things like disassembled furniture, mattresses, bicycles, broken-down cardboard, bagged clothing, move-out piles, grills under 75 pounds with the propane tank removed, and drained lawnmowers. Emptied appliances are accepted. Items must be set at least three feet from your cart and clear of parked cars and overhead lines, and large loads can take three to five days to clear.

The list of what the town will not touch is where most Mooresville calls actually come from: no construction or contractor debris, no carpet, no bricks, concrete, rocks or dirt, no mulch, no pallets, no tires, no flammables, and no electronics, including TVs, computers and printers. Yard waste runs the day after your garbage day, with leaves and grass placed loose at the curb rather than bagged, and limbs kept under four feet long and four inches thick, stacked in one direction. Debris from a hired tree service is the tree service's problem, not the town's.

The Town's Dial-A-Dumpster is free to residents for up to seven days and is available all year except November 1 through January 1, but it will only take yard waste or household waste in a single load. Co-mingling the two, or adding carpet, shingles, siding, painted wood, glass, paint, electronics or construction debris, gets those items left behind at the site.

The county side is the part most people run into last. Iredell County's drop-off sites, including the Mooresville transfer station on Macleod Drive and the Twin Oaks facility, are residents-only and require a current Iredell County solid waste decal with proof of residency. Non-residents attempting to dispose of material face a $50 fine per occurrence, and up to five tires per year are free at the transfer station only, rims removed. That residency rule is exactly why an out-of-county hauler's disposal route looks different from yours, and why the trip fee and load pricing reflect where the material can legally go rather than the nearest gate.

Which Mooresville neighborhoods do you work in, and what kind of work does each one generate?

Work runs from the downtown Main Street blocks out to the water, and the type of job changes noticeably with the housing stock. Lakefront addresses in The Point, Lakeside on Brawley, Mallard Head, Atwater Landing and the older waterfront pockets tend to produce dock and shoreline material: waterlogged foam floats, rotted dock boards, retired boat lifts and jet ski lifts, cracked kayaks, deck furniture that did not survive a winter, and basement or boathouse cleanouts. Those homes frequently sit at the bottom of long, steep, curving driveways, which means loads get carried or shuttled uphill rather than backed straight down to the door. That is not a reason to say no, it is a reason to describe the driveway honestly when you book so the right trailer comes.

Newer subdivisions off Waterlynn, Curtis Pond, Byers Creek, Bridgeport, Morrison Plantation, Stafford at Langtree and the growth around Exit 31 and Exit 36 lean toward move-in and move-out labor, flat-pack furniture builds, garage organization, playsets, treadmills and TV mounting on new drywall. Downtown's older homes near Main Street bring narrower lots, tight stairwells, real crawlspaces and attic clean-outs, plus the occasional alley-access constraint that decides whether a 16ft enclosed trailer can even get close.

Mooresville also has a heavy share of second homes, seasonal rentals and short-term rentals around the lake, which produces turnover work on a schedule: a whole living room replaced between guests, mattresses swapped, appliances rotated out. Many neighborhoods here run active HOAs with rules about how long anything can sit at the curb and where a trailer may stage, so a scheduled slot is worth more than a same-day scramble.

The Talbert Road and US 21 industrial and race shop corridor gives the town its other flavor of work entirely: shop and fabrication spaces clearing out benches, shelving, scrap and office furniture. Motorsports is not a hobby here, it is a payroll, and shop clean-outs move on a race calendar.

What does TV mounting cost in a Mooresville or Lake Norman home?

TV mounting is flat rate: $99 for sets up to 60 inches, $149 for 61 to 84 inches, $239 for 85 to 95 inches, and from $349 for 96 inches and up, which is a two-person minimum. The Zone 4 trip fee of $89 still applies in Mooresville.

The mount that comes up constantly in lake-area great rooms is the one over a stone or brick fireplace, often on a two-story chimney chase. That work starts from $199 because masonry is a different job than drywall and studs. If you are hanging more than one set on the same visit, each additional TV takes 25% off the mount labor.

Add-ons are never discounted and are priced separately so nothing is a surprise at the end: stick-on cord concealment is $39, in-wall cord concealment starts at $89, a flat mount is $40 and a full-motion mount is $65. Pricing assumes you already have your own bracket unless you buy a mount through us.

What do moving labor, handyman hours and flooring cost in Mooresville?

Hourly work is $70/hr for a solo handyman and $135/hr for a two-person crew, both with a two-hour minimum, billed in 30-minute increments after that. If the job needs a trailer on site, the utility trailer adds $35/hr and the 16ft enclosed adds $60/hr, so a two-person crew with the enclosed trailer is $195/hr. That combination is what usually handles a partial move, a garage-to-storage shuffle or a rental turnover.

Weekends work differently and it is worth knowing before you book. On Saturday and Sunday the two-person hourly crew carries a three-hour minimum, while the solo handyman stays at the two-hour minimum. Flat-rate services like TV mounting and junk removal have no minimum at all. Sunday adds 15% to flat-rate work only and never to hourly crew work.

Flooring is priced by the square foot: $2.50/sq ft for LVP and laminate, $3.50/sq ft for herringbone and premium layouts, $1.00/sq ft to tear out and haul the old floor, and $70/hr for subfloor prep when a slab or plywood needs correcting first. There is a minimum of 200 square feet or $500. Tear-out and haul is worth pricing in around here, since old carpet is on the town's do-not-collect list and has to leave the property some other way.

A few things that apply across all of it: stairs add $25 per flight above the first, a heavy or awkward item starts at +$40, and same-day is +$40 when it can be done. Card payments carry a 3.5% processing fee; cash, check and Zelle are also accepted. The Friends and Neighbors referral program gives the referrer a $25 credit and takes $25 off the new customer's first booking, and three booked referrals earns a $100 credit or a free hour of solo labor.
