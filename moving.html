What kinds of moves do you handle, and what falls outside that?

Mount N Muscle is local moving help, not a long-distance van line. The work that fits best is loading or unloading a rental truck or a PODS-style container, moving furniture around inside a house, apartment and single-room moves, one heavy item, and runs to or from a storage unit. Those are jobs where two people, dollies, straps and the right vehicle solve the problem.

A full interstate household move is not what this is. We do not run long-distance transport of household goods, we do not do binding estimates on a whole-house tonnage basis, and we do not broker a semi to another state. If you are moving from Charlotte to Denver, hire a van line or a container company for the transport and hire us for the part they charge the most for, which is the loading.

Packing is not a separate service either. If you want the crew boxing up a kitchen or wrapping a dresser, say so up front and it goes into the quote. It is usually cheaper for you to pack the small stuff yourself in the days before and save the crew for what actually needs two backs.

What changes the price of a move?

The quote is built from what you tell us, so the more exact the description, the closer the price holds. Bedroom count is the starting point, but it is not the whole story: a fully boxed one-bedroom by the door and a one-bedroom with open closets and loose kitchen cabinets are two different jobs.

The quote assumes everything is packed and ready to go when the crew arrives. If it is not, or if more turns up than was described, that gets said out loud and the new number is confirmed with you before the crew keeps going. We stand by the price; it only changes if the job does.

The other things that change a move are stairs, long carries, and disassembly. Every flight of stairs above the first adds $25. A long carry, meaning a walk of a hundred feet or more from the door to the truck because the loading zone is around the building, is real extra work on a mid-size apartment. Bed frames, sectional couches, treadmills, cribs and IKEA wardrobes all have to come apart and go back together, and a single bulky sectional with hardware nobody can find can eat 45 minutes.

When you call, describe the job in those terms and the quote gets much closer: bedroom count, floor number, elevator or stairs, distance from the door to where the truck can park, how packed you already are, and which items need tools.

What should I do before the crew arrives?

Everything on this list is free, and it is what keeps the move at the price you were quoted. Being fully boxed before arrival is the single biggest thing you control.

Pack and close every box, then stack them in one staging area near the exit door rather than spread across rooms. Label by destination room, not by contents. Empty dresser drawers if the dresser is going down stairs. Strip beds and bag the linens. Take down anything hanging on a wall, including TVs, mirrors and shelving, unless you booked the crew to do it. Disconnect and drain the washer, unplug the fridge the night before so it can defrost, and empty the ice maker.

Clear the path. Mats and rugs in a hallway are a trip hazard with a dresser on a dolly. Pets should be in a closed room or off site, and small children should not be in the carry lane. If the driveway is tight, move your own cars out first so the truck can back in.

Set aside a do-not-load pile in a bathroom or a closet with the door shut. Phone chargers, medication, passports, laptops, keys, cleaning supplies and anything you need that night should not end up in the back of a truck. Finally, decide before the crew arrives what is going and what is being left, so the load matches the quote.

Can you load a rental truck, PODS container or storage unit?

Yes, and that is the single most common job this crew does. We can load a truck or container you have already rented, or unload one that just arrived at your new place, and you keep control of the transport and the rental contract.

Professional loading matters more on a container or a truck than most people expect, because the load has to survive being driven. Weight goes low and forward, against the front wall, with heavy furniture and appliances as the base tier. Boxes stack heaviest on the bottom in tight columns to the ceiling so nothing can shift into open air. Mattresses, headboards, table tops and mirrors go flat against a wall, not loose in the middle. Soft goods and pillows fill the gaps. Everything gets tied off in tiers with straps so the front third does not slide back into the door when the driver brakes on I-485. A load packed loose by two friends on a Saturday is how dressers arrive with the drawers off the rails.

Storage unit runs are the same skill in reverse. Loading a unit with an aisle down the middle and the things you will need first at the front saves you a second rental fee later. If you do not have a truck at all, we bring the vehicle too.

In-home moves count too. Rearranging a living room, moving a bedroom set upstairs, getting a sleeper sofa into a basement or clearing a room for flooring installation is a normal small job, quoted the same way.

What should I know about moving into a Charlotte apartment or high-rise?

Call your building office at least a week ahead. Most managed communities in Uptown, South End, Dilworth, Ballantyne and the Steele Creek corridor require you to reserve the freight elevator in a set block, often two to four hours, and they only allow one move at a time. Showing up without that reservation can mean sitting in the lobby while another tenant's move finishes.

Ask the office four questions specifically: what hours moves are allowed, whether the freight elevator needs to be reserved and for how long, where the loading zone is and how long a vehicle may sit in it, and whether the building requires a vendor certificate of insurance. Many Charlotte buildings restrict moves to weekdays or to daytime windows and prohibit them on Sundays or holidays, and some cut off at 5pm.

On the certificate of insurance question, settle the paperwork before you book: ask the building exactly what it requires from a vendor and tell us when you call, so the paperwork is handled before move day instead of at a security desk.

Loading zones are the other Charlotte-specific squeeze. In dense South End and Uptown buildings the zone may hold one vehicle for a limited window, may sit on the far side of the building from your unit, or may be a parking deck with a height limit that a box truck cannot clear. A long carry from a distant zone changes the job, so tell us where the zone actually is when you book and we will plan the trip and the equipment around it.
