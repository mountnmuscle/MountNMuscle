How much does junk removal cost in Charlotte, NC?

A single item is $95, and that means one item one person can carry, from ground level — a garage, a driveway, a curb or a first floor — that fits the trailer as it sits. How do I know how much of the trailer my junk will fill?

An eighth of the 16ft trailer is about one pickup-bed worth of stuff, and that tier is $150. Most people badly underestimate a cleanout and badly overestimate a couch, so here is what each tier of the 16ft enclosed trailer actually looks like in practice.

If the estimate lands between two tiers, the lower tier gets quoted and only moves up if the load genuinely fills past the line. Photos sent by text get the closest answer without anyone driving out. Which items cost extra to haul, and why?

Four categories carry a surcharge because the disposal site charges for them by the piece: mattresses and box springs are +$25 each, refrigerant appliances are +$40, tires are +$8 each, and paint, electronics and other regulated items are passed through at whatever the disposal facility charges. Nothing is marked up on top of that.

The refrigerant fee is not arbitrary. Under EPA Section 608 rules, refrigerant has to be recovered by a certified technician before a fridge, freezer, window AC unit or dehumidifier can be scrapped or landfilled, and the last party in the disposal chain is the one on the hook for proving it happened. That recovery step is what the $40 covers.

Electronics are a state-law issue rather than a preference. North Carolina bans televisions, computers, monitors, printers, scanners and most computer peripherals from landfills statewide, so those items go to a registered electronics recycler instead of the transfer station. Tires are banned from NC landfills too and go to a tire processor by the each, which is where the $8 comes from.

Access add-ons are separate from material surcharges. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, a heavy or awkward single item starts at +$40, same-day or emergency scheduling is +$40, and Sunday adds 15 percent to flat-rate work. A third-floor walk-up apartment with a sleeper sofa is the classic case where both apply, and both get said out loud before the job is booked.

What will you not haul away?

Hazardous and regulated waste is a hard no, with no exceptions and no surcharge that makes it a yes. That list is hazardous chemicals, solvents, pesticides, motor oil, fuel, asbestos, lead paint waste, medical or biohazard waste, ammunition, explosives and pressurized tanks. Mecklenburg County residents can take most household hazardous waste to a county full-service recycling center instead.

Concrete, dirt and brick can be hauled, but they are priced separately by weight rather than by trailer space, because a quarter-trailer of masonry weighs more than a full trailer of furniture. Ask before the day of the job so the right trailer and the right disposal site get lined up.

Paint, electronics, tires and refrigerant appliances are all fine to include with the surcharges listed above. One thing worth knowing before the truck pulls away: once a load leaves the property, items cannot be retrieved. Check drawers, check the shed, and pull anything sentimental out of the pile first.

  • Cannot haul: asbestos, lead paint waste, medical or biohazard waste
  • Cannot haul: ammunition, explosives, pressurized tanks
  • Priced by weight, ask first: concrete, dirt, brick
  • Can haul with a surcharge: paint, electronics, tires, refrigerant appliances

Should I use Charlotte bulky item pickup or hire a junk removal service?

If the items are already at the curb, you have city trash service, and you can wait for the scheduled date, city bulky item pickup is free and worth using. Requests go through 311, the CLT+ app or servicerequest.charlottenc.gov, items go out by 6 a.m. on the collection day, and they need about three feet of space between them.

The catch is what the city leaves behind. Charlotte bulky collection will not take concrete, drywall, shingles, brick, cinderblock, ceramic tile or roofing material, will not take paint, fire extinguishers, propane, oxygen or kerosene tanks, and will not take debris generated by a commercial contractor. Tires are capped at eight per collection and only with the rims removed. Central air and heating units are excluded, though window units, washers, dryers, stoves and TVs are accepted.

Mecklenburg County also runs four full-service recycling and disposal centers, open Monday through Saturday from 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., that accept bulky waste, electronics and household hazardous waste from county residents. Those are Compost Central at 140 Valleydale Road, Foxhole at 17131 Lancaster Highway, Hickory Grove at 8007 Pence Road, and North Mecklenburg at 12300 N. Statesville Road in Huntersville. Restrictions apply and you still need a vehicle, a helper, and an afternoon.

Hiring makes sense when the junk is not already at the curb, when it is in a basement, attic, crawlspace or second-floor unit, when the load mixes materials the city refuses, when an HOA does not allow a pile sitting out for days, or when the address is outside Charlotte city collection entirely. A single item is $95 and it is off the property the same visit.
