How much does junk removal cost in Charlotte, NC?

A single item is $95 — one item, one person can carry it, from ground level, that fits as it sits (a garage, a driveway, a curb or a first floor). Anything that takes two people to lift is a heavy or awkward item at +$40, and pianos, gun safes, hot tubs and slate pool tables are quoted separately rather than billed at the single-item rate. Beyond a single item, junk removal is priced by the space the load takes, not by the hour, at one price per trailer load, from $150 for a pickup-bed load up to $700 for a trailer packed full.

Every load gets sized the same way, no matter how big it is: text photos of the pile to 704-237-0164 and a flat estimate comes back the same day, and it is confirmed as a quote before anything gets loaded. The tier that gets quoted is the tier that gets billed, and if a load turns out bigger than described once the crew is on site, that gets said out loud and confirmed before anything moves. Is that price per load or for the whole job?

Every price on the ladder is one trailer load, one trip. The tiers describe how full the trailer gets, and the names are there to make that easy to picture — a job that fills the trailer twice is two loads, and it is quoted that way before anything is lifted, never discovered afterwards.

That matters most on the big end. The trailer packed full is $700 for that trip, not a flat price for clearing a whole house, because a full three-bedroom cleanout is often more than one trailer will hold. Send photos and the number of trips is part of the quote you get back.

The $95 minimum applies to every trip, including a second one. If a renovation is going to generate another pile next week, say so — coming back on a scheduled second run is normal, and it is cheaper to plan it than to add it.

What is on the ladder, and what gets quoted instead?

The prices above cover household and office goods — furniture, boxes and bags, appliances, mattresses, electronics, garage and yard clutter, storage and estate contents. That is the great majority of what comes out of a house, and for that work the space it takes is a fair way to price it.

Dense material is quoted instead, and it is worth explaining why rather than just saying no. A trailer has an axle rating, and heavy material reaches that rating long before it fills the space. Broken concrete and brick run over a ton per cubic yard, so a pile of masonry that looks like a quarter load is already a full trip — the trailer is legally full at ankle depth. Pricing that off a volume ladder would be wrong in both directions: it would overcharge a small clean load and badly undercharge a heavy one. So it gets its own number, and the right trailer gets brought for it.

Quoted rather than priced off the ladder: concrete, brick, block and pavers · dirt, sod and gravel · roofing tear-off · drywall and demolition debris · construction and job-site waste · tile and plaster tear-out. We do take this work — demolition and contractor haul-off is a real part of what we do, not an exception we are trying to dodge. It just gets a real number first.

Two other things get quoted rather than laddered. Oversized and awkward single items — anything outside the normal run of household furniture, from a piano or gun safe to a hot tub, pool table or riding mower. And teardown work: sheds, playsets, decks, hot tubs and above-ground pools are quoted flat for the job, never by the hour.

Asbestos is the one thing we will not take at any price. It requires a licensed abatement contractor and a state permit, and no price makes that our job. Hazardous chemicals, fuel, solvents, pesticides, propane and pressurized tanks, ammunition and medical or biohazard waste are also out. Hoarding conditions, animal waste and biological cleanup are quoted separately rather than refused — tell us what it is and we will give you a number.

Which items cost extra to haul, and why?

Four categories carry a surcharge because the disposal site charges for them by the piece: mattresses and box springs are +$25 each, refrigerant appliances are +$40, tires are +$8 each, and paint, electronics and other regulated items are passed through at whatever the disposal facility charges. Nothing is marked up on top of that.

The refrigerant fee is not arbitrary. Under EPA Section 608 rules, refrigerant has to be recovered by a certified technician before a fridge, freezer, window AC unit or dehumidifier can be scrapped or landfilled, and the last party in the disposal chain is the one on the hook for proving it happened. That recovery step is what the $40 covers.

Electronics are a state-law issue rather than a preference. North Carolina bans televisions, computers, monitors, printers, scanners and most computer peripherals from landfills statewide, so those items go to a registered electronics recycler instead of the transfer station. Tires are banned from NC landfills too and go to a tire processor by the each, which is where the $8 comes from.

Access add-ons are separate from material surcharges. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, a heavy or awkward single item starts at +$40, same-day or emergency scheduling is +$40, and Sunday adds 15 percent to flat-rate work. A third-floor walk-up apartment with a sleeper sofa is the classic case where both apply, and both get said out loud before the job is booked.

What will you not haul away?

Hazardous and regulated waste is a hard no, with no exceptions and no surcharge that makes it a yes. That list is hazardous chemicals, solvents, pesticides, motor oil, fuel, asbestos, lead paint waste, medical or biohazard waste, ammunition, explosives and pressurized tanks. Mecklenburg County residents can take most household hazardous waste to a county full-service recycling center instead.

Concrete, dirt and brick can be hauled, but they are priced separately by weight, not by load size, because a small amount of masonry can weigh more than a full load of furniture. Ask before the day of the job so the right equipment and the right disposal site get lined up.

Paint, electronics, tires and refrigerant appliances are all fine to include with the surcharges listed above. One thing worth knowing before the truck pulls away: once a load leaves the property, items cannot be retrieved. Check drawers, check the shed, and pull anything sentimental out of the pile first.

  • Cannot haul: ammunition, explosives, pressurized tanks
  • Priced by weight, ask first: concrete, dirt, brick

Should I use Charlotte bulky item pickup or hire a junk removal service?

If the items are already at the curb, you have city trash service, and you can wait for the scheduled date, city bulky item pickup is free and worth using. Requests go through 311, the CLT+ app or servicerequest.charlottenc.gov, items go out by 6 a.m. on the collection day, and they need about three feet of space between them.

The catch is what the city leaves behind. Charlotte bulky collection will not take concrete, drywall, shingles, brick, cinderblock, ceramic tile or roofing material, will not take paint, fire extinguishers, propane, oxygen or kerosene tanks, and will not take debris generated by a commercial contractor. Tires are capped at eight per collection and only with the rims removed. Central air and heating units are excluded, though window units, washers, dryers, stoves and TVs are accepted.

Mecklenburg County also runs four full-service recycling and disposal centers, open Monday through Saturday from 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., that accept bulky waste, electronics and household hazardous waste from county residents. Those are Compost Central at 140 Valleydale Road, Foxhole at 17131 Lancaster Highway, Hickory Grove at 8007 Pence Road, and North Mecklenburg at 12300 N. Statesville Road in Huntersville. Restrictions apply and you still need a vehicle, a helper, and an afternoon.

Hiring makes sense when the junk is not already at the curb, when it is in a basement, attic, crawlspace or second-floor unit, when the load mixes materials the city refuses, when an HOA does not allow a pile sitting out for days, or when the address is outside Charlotte city collection entirely. A single item is $95 and it is off the property the same visit.
