What will the Town of Stallings pick up at the curb, and what won't it?

The town's contracted hauler collects bulk items only about three times a year, with a limit of four items per household per event, set out on your normal yard waste day during the announced bulk week. Extra pickups outside those weeks have to be arranged directly with the hauler at your own expense, which is where a private haul often costs less than waiting a quarter for a slot.

Even during a bulk week, the prep rules eliminate a lot of what people actually want gone. Most items are capped around 75 pounds. Carpet has to be rolled into four-foot lengths under 25 pounds per roll. Plywood is limited to roughly an inch thick and four feet square. Refrigerators and freezers need the doors removed. Bed frames get dismantled, grills need the propane tank pulled, cast iron tubs are refused outright, only wood fencing is taken, and nails have to be flattened for the crew's safety.

The categories the town does not take at all are the ones that come up constantly in a growing suburb: construction debris, contractor materials, bricks, concrete, drywall, roofing, toilets, pallets, auto parts and hazardous waste. Yard waste runs on its own biweekly schedule with clear biodegradable or paper bags only, capped at 25 thirty-two-gallon bags or 12 sixty-four-gallon bags, and branches must be under four inches across, four feet long and 50 pounds per bundle. If your project produced a bathroom vanity, an old toilet and a pile of demo drywall, none of that is going curbside.

Why does it matter that Stallings sits in two counties?

Stallings was the first Union County town to lie in two counties, and a portion of its addresses, mostly on the Idlewild Road side toward Charlotte and Mint Hill, fall inside Mecklenburg. That single fact changes where your junk can legally go, which is why two neighbors on opposite sides of a line can get different answers from the same hauler.

Union County's residential waste and recycling convenience centers are for Union County residents and are open Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays only, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Larger loads and oversized cardboard go to the Union County landfill on Austin Chaney Road in Monroe, which runs weekday hours only and charges by load. If your Stallings address is on the Mecklenburg side, those Union sites are not your facility, and county-run disposal and recycling centers on the Charlotte side, like the one on Pence Road in Hickory Grove, are the relevant option instead.

We sort that out before quoting rather than after loading. It affects routing, the disposal cost passed through on paint and electronics, and whether a Saturday drop is even possible. It also affects timing: a Tuesday or Thursday job on the Union side can hit a convenience center the same afternoon, while a Monday load may sit until the landfill opens.
