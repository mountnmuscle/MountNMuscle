How much does junk removal cost in Concord, NC?

Junk removal in Concord starts at $95 for a single item, plus the $89 Zone 4 trip fee that applies to every job in Concord. Concord is the outer edge of the service area and the run up I-485 to I-85 is a real drive, so that fee is stated up front rather than buried at the end of an estimate.

From there the price is set by how much of a trailer you fill. A 5x8 utility trailer is $145 quarter full, $210 half, $285 packed. A 6x10 utility trailer is $170 quarter, $230 half, $335 packed. The 16ft enclosed trailer is priced in eighths: $150 for 1/8, $235 for 1/4, $315 for 3/8, $395 for 1/2, $475 for 5/8, $560 for 3/4 and $700 full. Surcharges are added at the item level, not guessed at: mattresses and box springs are +$25 each, any refrigerant appliance such as a fridge, freezer or window AC unit is +$40, tires are +$8 each, and paint and e-waste are billed at whatever the disposal site charges.

Two Concord-specific cost notes. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, which matters in the older two-story homes off North and South Union Street and in the mill-village houses with steep attic stairs. Heavy or awkward single items start at +$40, which is the realistic bucket for a gun safe, a treadmill out of a Christenbury bonus room, or a cast-iron tub coming out of a 1920s bathroom.

Will the City of Concord pick up my bulky items for free instead?

Often, yes, and you should use it before you pay anyone. The City of Concord collects bulky waste every other week on your recycling day, at no charge, up to 10 cubic yards of regular bulky waste, or 6 cubic yards of bulky plus 4 cubic yards of construction debris. Items go to the curb by 6 a.m., set about two feet clear of your carts.

The limits are where people get stuck. Individual items are capped at 65 pounds unless they are wheeled, which is why a refrigerator or a push mower is fine but a solid-oak armoire, a slate pool table or a full-size safe is not. Carpet and padding are limited to two collections a year, rolls must be tied and no longer than six feet. Tires are two collections a year, up to eight per collection, with rims removed. Mattresses and box springs are supposed to be bagged in plastic. Anything encased in concrete, or bolted into a concrete footing you have not dug out, is refused, so basketball goals, swing-set posts and mailbox posts frequently get left at the curb.

Loose bags of clothes, shoes and household odds and ends are not bulky waste and are supposed to go in your cart or to a donation center. Dirt, brick, stone, concrete and rock are not collected curbside at all. If your pile is genuinely over the limit, Waste Pro sells roll-off container service directly. The point of hiring a hauler in Concord is usually one of three things: the pile is over 10 cubic yards, the items are over 65 pounds or concrete-set, or you cannot wait for the every-other-week cycle because of a closing date or a lease end.

Where can Concord residents legally take furniture, appliances and old TVs?

Cabarrus County runs its construction and demolition landfill at 4441 Irish Potato Road in Concord, open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Residential loads are charged by size: about $6 for up to five 32-gallon bags, $9 for six to ten, $15 for a pickup or trailer up to eight feet, and $24 if you have sideboards over 20 inches. Bring recyclables with you and you can knock about $5 off. The site takes cards only, no cash.

Several things Concord residents assume cost money are actually free there: scrap metal and appliances, power lawn equipment once it has been drained of fuel and oil, electronics including computers, TVs and cell phones, and household tires in loads of five or fewer. Christmas trees are free in January. Electronics are landfill-banned in North Carolina, which is why the old plasma TV in your garage cannot simply go in a cart, though the city will take e-waste and circuit-board items at the curb on bulky day.

The rule that catches people out is scope. The county's residential drop-off is built for household loads, not turnouts. Residential waste is not accepted in trailers longer than eight feet or in box trucks, and house and rental cleanouts are excluded from the residential program. That is precisely the gap a full 6x10 or a loaded 16ft enclosed trailer falls into, and it is a real part of what you are paying a hauler to sort out rather than discovering it yourself at the scale house on a Saturday morning.

Do you mount TVs in Concord, and what does it cost?

Yes. TV mounting in Concord is $99 for sets up to 60 inches, $149 for 61 to 75 inches, $199 for 76 to 85 inches, and from $299 for 86 inches and up, which is a two-person minimum job. The $89 Zone 4 trip fee applies here too, so a single small TV in Concord is a $188 visit before any add-ons.

Mounting over a fireplace or into stone or brick starts at $199. That comes up constantly in Concord: the older houses around Edgewood and the Union Street districts have real masonry chimneys and plaster, and the newer Christenbury and Moss Creek builds put a stone-veneer fireplace in the great room with the outlet in the wrong place. Cord concealment is priced separately and never discounted: stick-on channel is $39, in-wall routing starts at $89. A flat mount is $40 and a full-motion mount is $65 if you need one; otherwise prices assume you already have your own bracket.

If you have more than one TV, do them in the same visit. Each additional TV on the same trip is 25% off the mount labor, and since you are only paying the $89 trip fee once, a two- or three-TV visit is where Concord customers get the most out of the drive. Flat-rate work like TV mounting has no hourly minimum, but Sunday bookings add 15% to flat-rate services.

What kind of work do Concord homes and neighborhoods actually generate?

Concord is really three different housing stocks stacked into one city, and each one produces a different kind of job. Downtown, the North Union, South Union and Edgewood districts hold late-1800s and early-1900s Victorians and four-squares with narrow stairs, tight landings, plaster walls and detached garages full of decades of storage. Around them sit the mill villages built for Cannon and the other operations: Gibson Mill Village, Coleman-Franklin-Cannon, Odell-Locke-Randolph and Brown-Norcott. Those are small, well-built houses with crawlspaces, back sheds and original hardwood under two or three layers of later flooring. Tear-out and haul is $0.50 per square foot, and new LVP or laminate is $2.50 per square foot, herringbone or premium $3.50, with a 200 square foot or $500 minimum.

Then there are the mid-century pockets like Beverly Hills, Highland Park, Logan, West Concord and Wil-Mar Park, where the work skews to estate and downsizing cleanouts, garage clearing, appliance swaps and pre-listing haul-offs. And then the growth side facing Charlotte and the airport: Christenbury, Moss Creek, Afton Village and the newer subdivisions off Poplar Tent and around Afton Ridge and Concord Mills. Those homes are large, HOA-governed and turn over often, which means move-in and move-out labor, garage and bonus-room clearing, and furniture that has to be broken down to get out of a bonus room it was assembled in.

Concord also has commercial work that most cities do not. The corridor around Charlotte Motor Speedway and off Highway 49 is dense with race shops, fabrication space and small warehouses, and that produces shop cleanouts, scrap runs, pallet and crate removal, and office and breakroom furniture haul-off. Small commercial hauling is quoted the same way as residential: by trailer volume, plus the trip fee, plus $3.00 per loaded mile beyond the first ten if the disposal run is long.

HOA rules are worth planning around. In the newer Concord subdivisions, covenants typically limit how long bulk items can sit at the curb, so setting a pile out early to wait for the every-other-week city run can earn a letter. If your HOA is strict, the cleaner sequence is to keep everything in the garage and clear it in one visit.

How soon can you get to Concord, and how should I schedule it?

Schedule ahead. Concord is roughly a 35-mile run from Steele Creek, up I-485 and out I-85, and same-day service is unlikely there even though same-day is available for $40 in closer zones. Booking a day or more out is the honest answer, and it is the answer that gets you a real arrival window instead of a guess.

Because the $89 trip fee is charged per visit, batching pays. If you have a junk load, two TVs to mount and a handyman list, running them on one visit means one trip fee instead of three. Flexibility on the date helps too: if your job can float to a day already routed toward Cabarrus County, it is easier to fit and easier to schedule at a decent hour.

Hourly labor is $70 per hour for a solo handyman with a 2-hour minimum, or $135 per hour for a two-person crew with a 2-hour minimum, billed in 30-minute increments after that. Add a utility trailer for +$25 per hour or the 16ft enclosed for +$60 per hour, so a two-person crew with the enclosed trailer is $195 per hour. On Saturday and Sunday the two-person hourly crew moves to a 3-hour minimum; the solo handyman stays at 2 hours. The Sunday 15% uplift applies only to flat-rate work such as TV mounting and junk removal, never to hourly crew time.

Payment is cash, check, Zelle or card, with a 3.5% processing fee on cards. There is also a referral program called Friends and Neighbors: send someone and you get a $25 credit while they get $25 off their first booking, and three booked referrals earns either a $100 credit or a free hour of solo labor. In a city like Concord, where the trip fee is real, neighbors coordinating jobs on the same street is genuinely the cheapest way to use the service.
