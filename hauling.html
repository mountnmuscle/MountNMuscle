How much does hauling and delivery cost in Charlotte, NC?

Hauling is billed at the crew rate plus a trailer add-on plus travel. Solo labor is $70 per hour with a two-hour minimum of $140, and a two-person crew is $135 per hour with a two-hour minimum of $270. The trailer add-on is +$35 per hour for a utility trailer and +$60 per hour for the 16ft enclosed trailer. That makes solo with the utility trailer $105 per hour and a two-person crew with the enclosed trailer $195 per hour. After the minimum is met, time is billed in 30-minute increments, so a job that runs two hours and twenty minutes is charged at two and a half hours, not three.

That structure is different from the junk removal page on purpose. Junk removal is priced by how much space your pile takes up, because the cost there is volume and disposal. Hauling is priced by time and distance, because the cost is the drive, the load, the unload and the fuel. A dresser carried down one flight and across Steele Creek and a dresser carried out of Mooresville and delivered to Rock Hill are the same volume and nowhere near the same job.

Add-ons are listed the same way they are quoted. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first. A heavy or awkward item such as a treadmill, a large appliance or an oversized sectional starts at +$40. Pianos, gun safes, hot tubs and slate pool tables are quoted separately and never go at the single-item rate. Same-day or emergency scheduling is +$40. Same-day work is often available but never guaranteed, so the earlier you call the better the odds.

Payment is cash, check or Zelle with no fee. Cards carry a 3.5% processing fee, which is the card processor's cut passed through rather than a markup. The Friends and Neighbors referral program pays a $25 credit to whoever refers you and takes $25 off your first booking.

How does travel factor into a hauling quote?

Travel is built into the number we quote — one price for the job, not a job price with a trip charge added on top. The price is confirmed before any work starts, and it accounts for the drive to pick up, the drive to drop off, and everything in between, across all 16 metro cities Mount N Muscle runs out of Steele Creek to serve.

A cross-metro run still burns close to an extra hour of driving and a tank's worth of fuel next to a ten-minute delivery two neighborhoods over, and that difference gets folded into the quoted number before the job is booked rather than tacked on afterward as a surprise. Call or text with the pickup address and the drop address, and the number that comes back already accounts for the distance between them.

Nothing about that changes what shows up on the invoice: it is still the crew rate, the trailer add-on if one is needed, and any surcharges discussed up front, all rolled into a single confirmed price before the trailer gets loaded.

If cost is the deciding factor on a long run, ask about going solo with the utility trailer at $105 per hour instead of a two-person crew. Plenty of hauls only need a second set of hands for a few minutes at each end. If you or someone at the pickup can help steady a piece through a doorway, the solo rate does the same work for less.

Do I need the open trailer or the enclosed one?

Only two things change the price, so that is all you have to decide. An open utility trailer adds $35 per hour to the crew rate; the 16ft enclosed trailer adds $60 per hour. Booking the enclosed box for a job the open trailer handles costs $35 for every hour of the job and buys you nothing.

The open trailer covers most of what people call a haul: a sofa, a mattress set, an appliance pair, a bedroom set, a long dining table, a sectional, sheets of plywood, lumber and fencing, or a materials run. The 16ft enclosed is for volume, for height, and for anything that cannot get wet or ride exposed on the interstate — multi-room loads, storage unit transfers, and freight that has to arrive dry.

Two runs with the open trailer are sometimes cheaper than one run with the enclosed, and sometimes they are not, because a second round trip adds time. That call gets made on the phone, not in your driveway. Describe the items or send photos and the trailer gets picked to fit the job, not to run up the clock. Photos help more than measurements here.

  • 16ft enclosed trailer: +$60/hr — multi-room loads, storage transfers, anything weather-sensitive
  • Two-person crew with the 16ft enclosed, as a package: $195/hr

Can you pick up a Facebook Marketplace or estate sale purchase?

Yes, and it is one of the most common hauling calls. Priced the same way as everything else here: crew rate, trailer add-on and travel, with a two-hour minimum of $140 solo or $270 for two people before the trailer add-on. A typical single-item marketplace pickup close to the Steele Creek base lands at the minimum. Have the seller's address, a phone number that actually answers and the pickup window ready when you call.

What you need to arrange on your end matters more on these jobs than on any other. Confirm the seller will be home and the item is paid for, because a locked gate or a no-show seller still burns billable time. Ask whether the item is already disassembled, especially bed frames, sectionals, treadmills and anything that came into the house through a door it no longer fits through. Ask whether the seller is willing to help at the pickup end, because plenty of estate sales and storage cleanouts hand you a piece sitting on the second floor with nobody to lift the other end.

Say up front if the item is on stairs at either address, since stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, and say if it is a treadmill or a large appliance, since those start at +$40. A piano, gun safe, hot tub or slate pool table is quoted separately. Say it before booking, not on arrival. Estate sales and auctions also tend to have hard pickup deadlines, so give as much lead time as you can rather than relying on a same-day slot that carries +$40 and is never promised.

Can you deliver furniture, appliances or building supplies the store will not?

Yes. Store delivery gaps are the other half of this service, and they run at the same $105 per hour solo with a utility trailer or $135 per hour for two people before the trailer add-on and travel. Big-box and outlet stores are the usual source: the item is in stock today but the delivery slot is nine days out, or the store only delivers to the garage, or the clearance floor model is sold as-is with no delivery at all.

Common versions of this call include a mattress set from a warehouse showroom, a washer or dryer from a scratch-and-dent floor, a grill or patio set that will not fit in an SUV, kitchen cabinets and countertops from a supply yard, pavers and bagged concrete from a home center, fencing and lumber, and mulch or soil by the scoop from a landscape supplier. If the store loads it, the trailer takes it. If the store will not load it, say so when you call so the right crew size shows up.

Old-item haul-away can ride the same trip. If the new fridge is going in and the old one is coming out, the removal side carries the junk surcharges: a refrigerant appliance is +$40, a mattress or box spring is +$25 each, and tires are +$8 each. Paint, e-waste and other regulated items are billed at whatever the disposal facility charges, with no markup invented on top.

What can you not haul, and what has to be quoted separately?

A trailer has a weight limit as well as a volume limit, and heavy material hits the weight limit long before the trailer looks full. Concrete, dirt and brick are priced separately by weight rather than by the hour or the trailer, so ask first. A small load of broken driveway concrete can weigh more than a full trailer of household furniture, and disposal for that material is charged by the ton at the facility.

Some things cannot be hauled at all, for legal and safety reasons: hazardous chemicals, solvents, pesticides, motor oil, fuel, asbestos, lead paint waste, medical or biohazard waste, ammunition, explosives and pressurized tanks. Mecklenburg and the surrounding counties run household hazardous waste drop-off programs for most of that list, and pointing you there is free.

Weather and load security decide the trailer more often than people expect. Anything upholstered, anything with an unsealed wood finish, a mattress, electronics, artwork or boxed household goods should ride in the 16ft enclosed trailer if the forecast is not clean or the route includes highway speed. Open trailers get strapped and tarped and that is enough for lumber, appliances in cartons, patio furniture and yard material, but a tarp on I-485 in the rain is not the same as four walls and a roof. The enclosed trailer costs +$60 per hour against the utility trailer's +$25, which is cheap next to a soaked sectional.
