How much does a garage cleanout cost in Charlotte?

By how much space the load takes, not by how long it takes. A full one-car garage usually runs $395, and a full two-car garage runs $475 to $560. A smaller job — a single wall cleared, or a one-car garage that is only partly loaded — lands at $235 to $315 instead. A single item on its own is $95.

That single-item price means one item one person can carry, from ground level, that fits as it sits. A garage is the room where that definition gets tested most, so it is worth being plain about: a treadmill, a chest freezer or an oversized sectional is a two-person item at +$40, and a riding mower, gun safe, hot tub, slate pool table or a shed and playset teardown is quoted separately rather than squeezed into the single-item rate.

Nothing about the price moves once it is quoted. There is no hourly minimum and no weekend minimum on a cleanout, because it is flat-rate work. Saturday costs the same as a Tuesday. Sunday adds 15 percent. Same-day and emergency scheduling adds $40, and that is said out loud before the job gets booked, not discovered on the invoice.

  • A pickup-bed load: $150 — a handful of items, or clearing one shelf
  • A small room: $235 — a small one-car garage, or one wall cleared
  • A large room: $315 — a one-car garage with shelving and yard equipment
  • A one-car garage: $395 — a typical full one-car garage
  • A partially cleared two-car garage: $475 — a two-car garage with a vehicle still parked in it
  • A full two-car garage: $560 — floor to ceiling on both sides
  • A trailer packed full: $700 — a garage plus a shed, or a garage as part of a whole-home cleanout

How do I estimate how big my garage cleanout will be?

Almost everyone underestimates a garage. The reason is that a garage stores upward — wall shelving, overhead racks, rafters, stacked totes — and the eye reads floor space rather than volume. A pickup-bed load is the base unit of comparison, and that tier is $150. Most garages hold four to six times that once everything comes off the shelves and out of the rafters.

The reliable shortcut is to picture the garage emptied into pickup-bed loads. A single-car garage with shelving down one side and a workbench is typically four loads, which lands at the one-car garage tier, $395. A two-car garage with both walls loaded and an overhead rack is six to eight loads, which is $475 to $560. If an estimate lands between two tiers, the lower tier is what gets quoted, and it only moves up if the load genuinely fills past the line while everyone is standing there.

Smaller garage jobs do not need the full ladder either. A single wall cleared, a short list of specific items, or a one-car garage that never got fully loaded typically lands at the small-room or large-room tier, $235 to $315. What shows up to do the job depends on what was described and how tight the driveway is, and it does not change the single-item floor of $95 either way.

Do I have to sort the garage before you get here?

No. Sorting is the part people put off for years, and it is not a prerequisite. Point at what stays and what goes and the rest is handled. The common version of this is a garage where one shelf and the tool chest are keepers and everything else is out, and that works fine as an instruction.

There are two things worth doing before the trailer backs in, and they take about ten minutes. Check the drawers and the tops of the cabinets, because once a load leaves the property nothing can be retrieved from it. And pull anything sentimental out of the pile first — the box of photographs that got put in the garage during a move is the single most common regret on a cleanout, and it never looks like anything from the outside.

If the garage is being cleared for a sale, a lease turnover or an estate, the useful move is to text photos from the doorway rather than trying to describe it. A flat number comes back the same day, the same day, and confirmed in person before anything is loaded, which matters when the person making the decision is not the person standing in the garage.

Is that price per load or for the whole job?

Every price on the ladder is one trailer load, one trip. The tiers describe how full the trailer gets, and the names are there to make that easy to picture — a job that fills the trailer twice is two loads, and it is quoted that way before anything is lifted, never discovered afterwards.

That matters most on the big end. The trailer packed full is $700 for that trip, not a flat price for clearing a whole house, because a full three-bedroom cleanout is often more than one trailer will hold. Send photos and the number of trips is part of the quote you get back.

The $95 minimum applies to every trip, including a second one. If a renovation is going to generate another pile next week, say so — coming back on a scheduled second run is normal, and it is cheaper to plan it than to add it.

What is on the ladder, and what gets quoted instead?

The prices above cover household and office goods — furniture, boxes and bags, appliances, mattresses, electronics, garage and yard clutter, storage and estate contents. That is the great majority of what comes out of a house, and for that work the space it takes is a fair way to price it.

Dense material is quoted instead, and it is worth explaining why rather than just saying no. A trailer has an axle rating, and heavy material reaches that rating long before it fills the space. Broken concrete and brick run over a ton per cubic yard, so a pile of masonry that looks like a quarter load is already a full trip — the trailer is legally full at ankle depth. Pricing that off a volume ladder would be wrong in both directions: it would overcharge a small clean load and badly undercharge a heavy one. So it gets its own number, and the right trailer gets brought for it.

Quoted rather than priced off the ladder: concrete, brick, block and pavers · dirt, sod and gravel · roofing tear-off · drywall and demolition debris · construction and job-site waste · tile and plaster tear-out. We do take this work — demolition and contractor haul-off is a real part of what we do, not an exception we are trying to dodge. It just gets a real number first.

Two other things get quoted rather than laddered. Oversized and awkward single items — anything outside the normal run of household furniture, from a piano or gun safe to a hot tub, pool table or riding mower. And teardown work: sheds, playsets, decks, hot tubs and above-ground pools are quoted flat for the job, never by the hour.

Asbestos is the one thing we will not take at any price. It requires a licensed abatement contractor and a state permit, and no price makes that our job. Hazardous chemicals, fuel, solvents, pesticides, propane and pressurized tanks, ammunition and medical or biohazard waste are also out. Hoarding conditions, animal waste and biological cleanup are quoted separately rather than refused — tell us what it is and we will give you a number.

  • Quoted: concrete, brick, dirt, roofing, drywall, demolition and construction debris
  • Quoted: oversized single items and anything outside the normal household range

What comes out of a garage that costs extra?

Four things carry a surcharge, and all four live in garages. Refrigerant appliances are +$40 each — the second fridge or the chest freezer in the corner is the most common cleanout add-on there is. Tires are +$8 each, mattresses and box springs are +$25 each, and paint, electronics and other regulated items are passed through at whatever the disposal facility charges, with nothing marked up on top.

The refrigerant fee is not arbitrary. Under EPA Section 608 rules the refrigerant has to be recovered by a certified technician before a fridge, freezer, window AC unit or dehumidifier can be scrapped, and the last party in the chain is the one who has to prove it happened. The tire fee exists because North Carolina bans scrap tires from landfills, so they go to a tire processor by the each. Electronics are the same kind of rule: NC bans televisions, computers, monitors, printers and most peripherals from landfills statewide, so a garage full of old tower PCs routes to a registered recycler.

One more is worth knowing about ahead of time, because it is new and it lives in exactly this room. North Carolina bans lithium-ion batteries from landfills effective December 1, 2026. Cordless tool packs, e-bike and scooter batteries, and old power-tool chargers all fall under it, and they are almost always in a garage rather than anywhere else in the house. Set them aside and they get routed correctly instead of turning into a problem at the transfer station.

Access charges are separate from material surcharges. Stairs are +$25 per flight above the first, which comes up on basement garages and on townhome units where the garage is a level below the street.

  • Refrigerant appliance (fridge, freezer, window AC, dehumidifier): +$40 each
  • Mattress or box spring: +$25 each
  • Tire: +$8 each
  • Paint, electronics and regulated items: passed through at disposal cost, no markup
  • Stairs above the first flight: +$25 per flight
  • Two-person heavy or awkward item (treadmill, large appliance, oversized sectional): +$40

What will you not take out of a garage?

Hazardous and regulated waste is a hard no, with no surcharge that turns it into a yes. In a garage that means motor oil, fuel and gas cans, solvents, pesticides and lawn chemicals, propane and other pressurized tanks, ammunition, and asbestos or lead paint waste. Those are the items most likely to be sitting on a garage shelf, so it is better to know before the trailer arrives than after.

There is a straightforward place for them. Mecklenburg County runs four full-service recycling centers that take household hazardous waste from county residents, open Monday through Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4 p.m.: Compost Central at 140 Valleydale Road, Foxhole at 17131 Lancaster Highway, Hickory Grove at 8007 Pence Road, and North Mecklenburg at 12300 N. Statesville Road in Huntersville. Paint, oil and chemicals all go there. York County residents in Fort Mill, Tega Cay, Lake Wylie and Rock Hill have a Household Hazardous Material collection event on the first Saturday of March and October.

Concrete, brick, dirt and old patio slab can be hauled, but they are priced by weight rather than by space, because a small amount of masonry can outweigh a full load of furniture. Bring it up when booking rather than on the day, so the right crew and the right disposal site are lined up.

  • Cannot haul: motor oil, fuel and gas cans, solvents, pesticides, lawn chemicals
  • Cannot haul: propane and pressurized tanks, ammunition, explosives
  • Cannot haul: asbestos, lead paint waste, medical or biohazard waste
  • Priced by weight, ask when booking: concrete, brick, dirt, patio slab
  • Fine to include with a surcharge: paint, electronics, tires, refrigerant appliances

Can you clear the garage if I am not there?

Yes, and it is a common way to run a cleanout for a rental turnover, an estate, or an out-of-state owner. The price gets confirmed in writing before anyone rolls, based on photos, so there is no situation where a number changes while nobody is on site to hear it.

What is needed is access and a clear line. A garage code or a lockbox covers access. The clear line is a text saying what stays — and if the answer is that nothing stays, that is the simplest job on the list. Photos go out when the trailer is loaded so the person who is not standing there can see the room.

Payment is due when the load is on the trailer. Cash, check and Zelle carry no fee; cards add 3.5 percent. For a remote job that usually means Zelle, which settles the same afternoon and costs nothing.
